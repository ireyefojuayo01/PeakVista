Featured Peak

Mount Everest (Sagarmatha)

Rising majestically on the border between Nepal and China, Mount Everest stands as the undisputed highest mountain in the world. Formed by tectonic forces shifting continents millions of years ago, it remains the ultimate destination for mountaineering expeditions.

Everest consists of multiple layers of limestone, marble, and pelite rock formed beneath primordial oceans, which were pushed upward over 50 million years ago during the ongoing collision between the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates.

The two main commercial pathways are the South Col route from Nepal and the North Ridge route from Tibet. Expeditions require months of heavy acclimatization to withstand the extreme thin air within the dangerous 'Death Zone' above 8,000 meters.

Highest Freestanding

Mount Kilimanjaro

Located in Tanzania, Kilimanjaro boasts the titles of both Africa's tallest peak and the tallest freestanding mountain system in the world. Because it doesn't belong to a traditional range, its snowy dome contrasts beautifully against the surrounding savannah.

Kilimanjaro is a massive volcanic massif composed of three distinct volcanic cones: Shira, Mawenzi, and Kibo. While Shira and Mawenzi are extinct, Kibo is dormant and could potentially erupt again in the future.

Unlike Everest, reaching Kilimanjaro's summit (Uhuru Peak) is a walk-up trek rather than a technical climb. Popular paths like the Machame and Marangu routes pass through five distinct ecosystem zones, from dense rainforest to arctic ice caps.

Cultural Icon

Mount Fuji (Fuji-san)

An exceptionally symmetrical stratovolcano located near Tokyo, Mount Fuji is the tallest mountain peak in Japan and a deeply sacred cultural symbol. It has inspired centuries of art, poetry, and spiritual pilgrimage treks.

Fuji sits on a complex triple junction of tectonic plates. Although classified as an active volcano, it has remained completely dormant since its last major historical event, the great Hoei eruption of 1707.

Hundreds of thousands of travelers climb Fuji every summer during July and August. Most hikers begin climbing late at night from the 5th Station, reaching the snow-line ridge just in time to witness Goraiko—the famous spiritual sunrise.
